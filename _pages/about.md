I am a CANFAR-CTN+ postdoctoral research fellow based at the Research Institute of the McGill University Health Centre. Supervised by [Dr. Alexandra de Pokomandy](https://www.mcgill.ca/familymed/alexandra-de-pokomandy), my postdoctoral research focuses on quantifying the real-world uptake and impact of cardiovascular disease primary prevention interventions among people living with HIV in Quebec by utilizing causal inference methodology. As a trained biostatistician, I am passionate about utilizing, and developing if needed, statistical methods to address public health needs such that my work is pertinent to care providers and community members.

I previously completed my PhD in biostatistics at McGill University, under the supervision of [Dr. Erica Moodie](https://www.ericamoodie.com/). During my PhD, I developed methods for generating completely synthetic trial data that were adequately complex and realistic in order to inform optimal trial design. I also had the pleasure of collaborating with [Dr. Nicolas Savy](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nicolas-savy-b9b47819/) at the Institut de Mathématiques de Toulouse, where I not only learned about synthetic data methods but also became comfortable with the French language. Prior to that, I received my master's in biostatistics from the University of Washington in Seattle (my hometown!) and my undergraduate degree in mathematics from McGill.

When I am not tinkering with R code, I enjoy dancing, experimenting in the kitchen, playing board games with friends, and practicing different languages.
